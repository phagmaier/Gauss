Unit 2 · The derivative

The derivative at a point

$$f'(a)=\lim_{h\to 0}\frac{f(a+h)-f(a)}{h}$$

The slope of the secant through $a$ and $a+h$, as $h$ shrinks to $0$. It exists only when this limit exists.

Example$f(x)=x^2$, $a=3$: $\dfrac{(3+h)^2-9}{h}=6+h\to 6$, so $f'(3)=6$.

The tangent line

$y=f(a)+f'(a)\,(x-a)$

Example$y=x^2$ at $a=3$: $y=9+6(x-3)=6x-9$.

Average and instantaneous rate

Average over $[a,b]$: $\dfrac{f(b)-f(a)}{b-a}$, for $b\ne a$. Instantaneous at $a$: $f'(a)$.

Example$s(t)=4.9t^2$ metres. On $[1,2]$: $\dfrac{19.6-4.9}{1}=14.7$ m/s. At $t=1$: $s'(1)=9.8$ m/s.

Differentiable, continuous

If $f'(a)$ exists, $f$ is continuous at $a$. Continuity alone does not give a derivative.

Example$|x|$ at $0$: the quotient $\frac{|h|}{h}$ is $-1$ for $h<0$ and $1$ for $h>0$, so no limit, no $f'(0)$, though $|x|$ is continuous there.

Notation

$f'(x)=\dfrac{dy}{dx}=\dfrac{d}{dx}f(x)$ is a function of $x$; $f'(a)$ is one number, its value at $a$.

Unit 3 · Differentiation rules

Powers, sums, constant multiples

$\dfrac{d}{dx}x^n=nx^{n-1}\qquad (af+bg)'=af'+bg'$

For a whole number $n$, at every $x$; for any real $n$, at $x>0$.

Example$(4x^3-2x+7)'=12x^2-2$; $\ (\sqrt{x})'=\dfrac{1}{2\sqrt{x}}$ for $x>0$.

Product

$(fg)'=f'g+fg'$

Example$(x^2\sin x)'=2x\sin x+x^2\cos x$

Quotient where $g(x)\ne 0$

$$\left(\frac{f}{g}\right)'=\frac{f'g-fg'}{g^2}$$

Example$\left(\dfrac{x}{x^2+1}\right)'=\dfrac{(x^2+1)-x\cdot 2x}{(x^2+1)^2}=\dfrac{1-x^2}{(x^2+1)^2}$

Chain

$\dfrac{d}{dx}f\bigl(g(x)\bigr)=f'\bigl(g(x)\bigr)\cdot g'(x)$

The outer derivative is taken at the inner value, then multiplied by the inner derivative.

Example$\bigl(\sin x^2\bigr)'=\cos(x^2)\cdot 2x$

Which rule first

  1. Name the last operation: a sum, a product, a quotient or a function of a function.
  2. Apply that rule, then the same steps to each piece.

Example$x\,e^{-x^2}$ is a product: $e^{-x^2}+x\cdot(-2x)\,e^{-x^2}=(1-2x^2)\,e^{-x^2}$.

Implicit differentiation

Differentiate both sides in $x$, with $y$ a function of $x$: each $y$ term brings a $\frac{dy}{dx}$. Solve for it.

Example$x^2+y^2=25$: $2x+2y\,\frac{dy}{dx}=0$, so $\frac{dy}{dx}=-\frac{x}{y}$ for $y\ne 0$. At $(3,4)$: $-\frac34$.

Inverse functions

$(f^{-1})'(b)=\dfrac{1}{f'(a)}$

Where $f(a)=b$ and $f'(a)\ne 0$.

Example$f(x)=x^3+x$: $f(1)=2$, $f'(1)=4$, so $(f^{-1})'(2)=\frac14$.

Standard derivatives

$\sin x$$\cos x$
$\cos x$$-\sin x$
$\tan x$$\sec^2 x$$\cos x\ne 0$
$e^x$$e^x$
$a^x$$a^x\ln a$$a>0$
$\ln x$$\dfrac1x$$x>0$
$\arcsin x$$\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{1-x^2}}$$|x|<1$
$\arctan x$$\dfrac{1}{1+x^2}$

Watch for

  1. The quotient's numerator starts with $f'g$. For $\frac{d}{dx}\frac{x}{x^2+1}$ you wrote $\frac{x^2-1}{(x^2+1)^2}$; it is $\frac{1-x^2}{(x^2+1)^2}$. Check: at $x=0$ the function rises, so the numerator is positive there.
  2. Take the outer derivative at the inner value: $(\sin x^2)'=\cos(x^2)\cdot 2x$, not $\cos(2x)$.
  3. Keep the inner derivative's sign: $(e^{-x^2})'=-2x\,e^{-x^2}$; you chose $2x\,e^{-x^2}$.
  4. $f'(3)$ is a number. For $f(x)=x^2$ you gave $2x$; evaluate it: $f'(3)=6$.
  5. A tangent can meet the curve again: the tangent to $y=x^3$ at $x=1$, $y=3x-2$, crosses it at $x=-2$.

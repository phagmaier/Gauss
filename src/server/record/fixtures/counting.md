# Three Ways of Counting

A short text for the dev server's seeded source course. It was written for
Gauss and is no one's book.

## The pigeonhole principle

Put more pigeons than there are holes into the holes, and some hole holds two
pigeons. Said like that, the principle sounds too plain to be of use. Its
force comes from choosing what the pigeons and the holes are.

Among any five points in a square of side two, some two lie within a
distance of the square root of two. Cut the square into four squares of side
one: those are the holes, the points are the pigeons, and two points in one
small square are no further apart than its diagonal.

The principle proves that something exists without saying where it is. That
is its strength and its limit: it finds the pair, but not which pair.

## Counting in two ways

Count one collection in two different ways, and the two counts must agree.
The equation that results often says something that is hard to prove
directly.

In a room where some people shake hands, add up the number of hands each
person shook. Every handshake is counted twice in that sum, once for each of
its two people, so the sum is even. It follows that the number of people who
shook an odd number of hands is even.

## Invariants

An invariant is a quantity that a process cannot change. If it differs
between where you start and where you want to end, no sequence of steps gets
you there.

Remove two opposite corners from a chessboard and ask whether dominoes can
tile what is left. Every domino covers one dark square and one light one,
but the two corners removed are the same colour. The difference between the
counts of dark and light squares is an invariant of placing dominoes, so the
tiling cannot exist.
